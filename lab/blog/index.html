A short argument

Open your calendar and look at the days that are circled.

Birthdays. Public holidays. The long weekend you’ve been counting down to since March. They’re easy to spot: they come with a reason, a plan and, usually, a reminder.

Now look at the days that aren’t. That’s most of them. And if we’re honest, that’s where most of the good stuff happens: the “I was just nearby,” the “thanks for covering me,” the “stay, have another one.”

We tend to save our small gestures for big occasions. This is a short argument for doing the opposite.

Tuesday · 4:12 pm

The moments we don’t plan

“I was just nearby.”

Mei Ling wasn’t expecting anyone. It was one of those afternoons without a name: no deadlines worth mentioning, no plans worth cancelling. Then her phone lit up.

I’m downstairs :)

She took the lift down in her house clothes. There was no occasion and no news to announce, just a quick hug in the lobby, a couple of Ferrero Rocher pressed into her hand and ten minutes that weren’t on anyone’s calendar. You don’t remember an afternoon like that for what happened. You remember it because someone showed up.

Try thisMessage first, plan later. “I’m downstairs” is a better opening than “Are you free?” It leaves no room for “maybe next week.”
Wednesday · 9:40 am

Small gestures, lasting feelings

“Thanks for covering me.”

Priya didn’t write a speech. She wrote one line on a sticky note and stuck it on Hafiz’s notebook, with a single Ferrero Rocher on top.

It cost almost nothing. It took under a minute. For Hafiz, it was the first thing he read all week that wasn’t a deadline.

Gratitude doesn’t need an audience or an anniversary. It needs to arrive while the favour is still fresh, which, conveniently, is nearly always a Wednesday.

Try thisKeep it small enough to accept and specific enough to mean something. “Thanks for covering me” beats “Thanks for everything.”
No one has ever said, ‘I wish they’d waited for a public holiday.’
Share a little love
Sunday · 7:30 pm

A little something, just because

“Stay a little longer.”

By Sunday evening the week has mostly happened. The teh was already poured when the plate came out: a few Ferrero Rocher, still in their gold, in the middle of the table.

Someone unwrapped one. Someone else said “one more story.” Dinner, which had been about to end, quietly turned into an evening.

That’s the thing about sharing something small: it gives people a reason to stay at the table a little longer, and it asks nothing of them except to stay.

Try thisPut something small in the middle of the table, not on one person’s plate. Sharing is the point.
Small enough to carry

The golden gesture

Every one of these moments has the same quiet co-star. Open one up: under the gold foil sits a crisp wafer shell, a creamy hazelnut filling and a whole hazelnut at the heart. It is a small piece of Ferrero Rocher that is easy to carry, easy to share and easy to say “this is for you” with.

Small enough to carry in a pocket. Gold enough to mean it.
